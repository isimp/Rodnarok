# Changelog

## 0.1.2

Each fish pulls the line by its own swimming speed in the game. A Perch fights as before; a Pike or a Tuna strains a held line sooner and takes more line when eased off, an Anglerfish or a Tetra less. A fish taking line swims away exactly as fast as the line goes out, so it can no longer drag the float past the line to where the game breaks it without warning. The new server setting `PullBySpeed` turns it off.

Wearing a *Wisplight*, its wisp guards your float. While the float waits it drifts round it, searching, and now and then dips low over the water; when a fish bites it dives in close and flares; through the fight it follows the float, and once the float is gone it comes back to you. Your mist clearing stays with you while the wisp is out, and the wisp clears a small pocket round the float. Other players see the wisp move but not the flare. The new setting `FloatWisp`, your own, turns it off.

Plain *Fishing Bait* also brings the fish that live where the float lands: in the Black Forest a *Trollfish* as often as a *Perch* or a *Pike*, in the Swamp a *Giant herring*, at sea a *Tuna*, a *Coral cod* or a *Pufferfish*, and so on. A biome's own bait still brings only its fish, anywhere, and the *Tetra* keeps to its *Cold Fishing Bait*. The new server setting `BiomeFishOnBait` turns it off.

Each fish tells itself on its way to your float by a sign of its own on the water, more often as it nears: a *Perch* small rings, a *Giant herring* a flicker of tiny rings, a *Pike* one swirl by the float, a *Trollfish* a heavy bulge, a *Grouper* big bubbles, a *Coral cod* a fizz, a *Pufferfish* swelling domes, an *Anglerfish* a lure glowing under the water, a *Magmafish* steam, a *Tuna* a boil of splashes, a *Northern salmon* a fin and a *Tetra* a zigzag of glints. Every player at the water sees them. Fish no longer all leap on their way in: only a *Tuna* or a *Northern salmon* does, and mostly at dawn and dusk, as the game's own fish leap.

A fast fish such as a *Pike* or a *Tuna* now slows as it closes on the bait, so it meets the hook and bites instead of circling it.

A seated cast goes where you aim instead of straight ahead. Sitting on the ground you turn toward your aim while drawing; on a chair or a bench the cast follows your aim up to 60 degrees to either side of the seat.

## 0.1.1

A struggling fish takes line slower while the line still holds strain: short taps on the block button that keep the ring partly filled lose less line than letting go. Each struggle writes one log line: the line the fish took, eased off and held time, and any hold the game did not count.
