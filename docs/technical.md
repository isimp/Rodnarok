# Rodnarök technical notes

## How the game fishes

These are the game's rules Rodnarök builds on, read from Valheim 1.0.20.

A fish picks a new place to swim every 5 to 15 seconds and, each time, takes a float within 50 m with a one in ten chance. It counts as arrived when it is level with the hook on one ground axis and in height, the other ground axis left out, so a float can dip with the fish still metres away. The bite is one downward push on the float, about as big as a wave, and holding the line hooks the fish only within half a second of it. A fish nibbling at the wrong bait never comes back to that float. Fish flee from the noise a player makes: walking reaches 15 m, running and jumping 30 m, building 50 m, fading by 4 m a second.

A hooked fish takes turns struggling and resting. Reeling costs the fish's stamina a second times its quality, far more while it struggles (a resting Perch 3, a struggling Northern Salmon 60), and Fishing skill lowers it to a fifth. While it struggles the line comes in at half speed, and at no stamina the fish is lost. The line breaks when the fish drags the float 10 m past the reeled length or 30 m from the rod; holding never strains it, so in the game holding is always safe. A struggle lasts half a second up to its fish's longest, 1.5 s more per star, and a rest a second or two up to five.

Every fish takes exactly one bait. Fish spawn only in their own biome, never inside a player base and never near a player; the Tetra lives only in mountain caves.

## What Rodnarök changes

The bait table is built at runtime from the baits the game's own fish take (`BaitTable`), so a fish another mod adds with a bait of its own comes too. A bait two fish take brings each in equal share.

A while after the float lands, the bait draws a school of its fish (`School`): as many as the world spawns of that fish together (a Perch comes in twos to sixes, a Northern Salmon in threes to tens), each size as likely, and two to four of a fish the world never spawns in groups, such as the cave fish. Each fish rolls its stars as the world's spawns do, and the game draws a fish 40% larger for each star. They leap on their way in, circle the float near the top of the depth they swim at, and one of them, each as likely, takes the bait, so the catch keeps the game's odds of stars. When it is hooked the others scatter. With `Schools` off a single fish comes. With `Bumps` on (off by default), the fish picked to take the bait bumps the float once or twice before it goes for the hook (`Bumps`): a pull of 12 m/s² for 0.25 s against the bite's 14 m/s² for a second, none sooner than 0.4 s after it is picked or later than 0.6 s before it goes, two at least 0.5 s apart. A bump is no nibble the game knows of, so reeling at it hooks nothing, and it drags the float, which startles a wary school.

Fish never leave the world by themselves in the game: each is kept in the world's save until caught. Rodnarök marks its own fish, takes them away out of sight once their float is gone, and any player's game takes away a marked fish it finds with no float, so none gather in the save. A fish of the world's own is never taken away. Rodnarök's fish are not moved by the game's own flight from noise, which would scatter a school the moment a player walks up; the world's own fish still flee from it. Instead a school is wary in a way of its own (`Wary`, setting `Wary`, on by default). Every frame, in the game that cast the float, the game's own noise rule is asked at the float and at the school's middle, with 35% of the range, as a fish hears under water: a player within that share of their noise (the game's walking 15 m, running and jumping 30 m, building 50 m, fading by 4 m a second, so about 5 m walking and 10.5 m running; sneaking, sitting and standing still make none) startles the school, which darts 6 m off the player at the game's fleeing speed and comes back once all has been quiet for 3 s. The float moving across the water faster than 0.6 m/s, measured over half seconds, while a fish of the school is within 6 m of it, as a short reel does, only makes the school flinch: a 2.5 m sidestep off the float, back once the float has lain still for 1.5 s. While off the float no fish of it goes for the bait. Frights add up over a cast, a startle 2 and a flinch 1, and at 8 the school leaves for good, diving out of sight as a school whose float is gone; so does it when the float keeps moving that fast for 2 s with the school near it or already flinched aside, as reeling it in does. A fish sidesteps straight away where the water stays deep enough for it, else turned 45° or 90° aside, and waits where it is where none of these is deep enough. The cast's own start is too soft to matter: an attack's 10 m around the one fishing has faded before a school comes near. Once a school is ready to bite it is shy as much as the shyer of two measures says: how close the one fishing stands across the ground (shyest within 4 m, not shy from 12 m) and how shallow the water is under the float (shyest under 1 m, not shy from 3 m). As it is first ready it gives up and swims off with a chance of 30% of its shyness, and otherwise hesitates up to 12 s, as long as its shyness, before a fish goes for the bait. Only the game that cast the float runs this, as it steers the school; another player is heard only by their noise reaching the float.

A bite needs the fish within reach of the hook through all three axes (`Bite`): its mouth within 0.6 m of the point a metre under the float where the game sends a fish going for it. A bite from further off is held back, and the fish tries again as it swims closer. A bite pulls the float under for about a second, beyond the one short push the game gives it. The hook window is a setting, 1.5 s by default. These apply to every fish at a float, a school's or the world's own. The crosshair ring (`Ring`, `RingDraw`, `CrosshairRing`) is one ring of one size from the bite to the end of the fight, made from the game's bow ring as a ring of its own, so it never looks like drawing the rod. After a bite its arc is full and white and drains clockwise toward the top, turning yellow, as the hook window runs out. While a hooked fish struggles the ring is red; while it rests, yellow. With tension its arc is the line's strain, filling clockwise from the top over a faint full track of the same colour (a second ring made the same way); without, the whole ring shows. While a bow or the rod is drawn it is hidden and the game's bow ring shows as ever.

Missed bites wear the bait down (`BaitLoss`, setting `BaitLost`, on by default). The game lets a fish bite the same bait again a second after a missed bite, for ever, and gives the bait back when the empty float is reeled in. In the game that owns the float, a bite neither hooked nor any longer within the hook window is a miss, and the fish that bit can no longer be hooked. Each miss on a float may take the bait, more likely each time: 10% for the first, 40% for the second, 90% for the third and 98% for every later one. Taken, the float is bare, a bite on it is refused, the game counts its bait as used so reeling it in gives none back, and its school, or the fish of the world's own called to it, moves on. A fish that missed has learned: its bites on that float are refused and the school sends another of its fish; once every fish of the school has missed, the school swims off and the bait is left, so reeling in gives it back; a fish of the world's own called to the float is the game's again after its one miss. A line at the top left says each time whether the fish only nibbled at the bait, nibbled while the school swam off, or took it. A bump is no bite and costs nothing.

Holding the line while the fish rests reels it in at the game's speed and cost. A struggling fish shows it at the surface (`Thrash`): the game wiggles it and now and then splashes with it, mostly unseen under the water; Rodnarök brings it up until its back breaks the surface and draws the game's splash for it about three times a second, on every player's screen, and stops when it rests; a hooked fish is held near the float in both. The game saves a struggle's length as a decimal number but its end as a whole 0, which it keeps apart, so every game, the game's own splashes included, went on reading a struggle after it ended; the game owning the fish now saves the end as a decimal too. While it struggles the line stalls: no line is gained, and it costs the `StallStamina` setting a second, the same for every fish, lowered by Fishing skill as reeling is (`Fight`).

Line tension is a rule of Rodnarök's own, since the game's line never strains (`Tension`, `Lines`, setting `Tension`, on by default). Holding the line through a struggle strains it, from none to snapped over `SnapSeconds` (1 s); a line strained to snapping breaks as the game breaks one, with the game's message and effect. Easing off lets the strain go over `EaseSeconds` (1 s), and while the fish rests it eases whether the line is held or not, so a rest never strains it. While the line is eased off in a struggle the fish takes line out at `RunSpeed` (2 m/s) and heads away from the one fishing, and past the game's 30 m the line breaks as ever. Only a short struggle can be held through; most, and every one of a fish with stars, need the line eased off once or more. The strain is kept by the game that cast the float, which runs the fight. Every line that breaks in the game that cast it is written to the log, saying whether the strain snapped it or the game's own rule broke it, with the line's length, the float's distance from the rod tip, the tip's height above the sea, the fish's distance from the float and whether it struggled, the strain, the hold, the stamina and whether the one fishing sat.

The water is calmed by the game's own *Item stand*, wall or horizontal, holding a Serpent trophy (`CalmStand`, `CalmStands`); Rodnarök adds no piece of its own. Put up, the trophy calms nothing. A tap of the use key on that stand switches its calm on or off: the game gives the plain tap on a stand holding an item no work, while holding the key still takes the trophy and the turn key still turns it. The switch is kept in the stand's own saved data under a key of Rodnarök's, flipped by the stand's owner at a player's call, so every player sees the same and it lasts; the stand's ward is asked first, as the game asks it, and a boss's stone, whose trophy cannot be taken, is left alone. Taking the trophy off switches the calm off, so the next trophy put up starts off. The stand's hover text says whether it calms the waters and offers the switch, the key named as the game names it; the words are plain text, since the game takes new words only through a private table it clears on a language change. Every game looks over its loaded stands twice a second, so a stand on a ship takes the calm along with that lag.

A calming stand lowers the waves through the corner values the game keeps for each 64 m water tile. The game shapes the waves of what floats and draws the water from the same four values, so both stay calm together, and a corner is lowered by where it is alone, so two tiles meeting at it always agree and no seam shows (`CalmWater`). The calmed area follows the tiles' corners and fades across a tile. A place lies at most 45 m from its tile's nearest corner, at the tile's middle, so the default reach of 48 m (`CalmReach`) calms at least one corner wherever a stand stands; near a tile's middle that corner's share of the waves there is small.

## Fishing while sitting

The game stands a seated player up on any attack or block press (`Player.SetControls`), cancels a drawn cast while the player sits on a seat (`Player.UpdateAttackBowDraw`), and lets nobody start an attack in the sitting pose (`Character.CanMove`). A player holding a rod (anything casting fishing bait) who sits on a chair, a bench or a boat's bench, or on the ground with the sit emote, and holds still, is seen as not seated by those three for the moment they ask (`SeatedFishing`), so the rod casts and reels and the player stays seated. Moving, jumping or crouching stands the player up as in the game. A ship's controls are not a seat for this.

The game plays its throw and its pull on the same base animation layer as the sitting pose, standing the whole body up for them, and its sitting down again after; the throw cannot be left out, since the cast leaves the rod at an event inside its animation. So every seated player's hips, spine and legs are kept as the seat has them (`SeatedBody`, `SeatedBodies`), whatever `SeatedMotions` says. A player sits for every game alike: the game turns on an animator switch for the seat, one named `attach_…` for a chair, a bench or a boat's bench and `emote_sit` on the ground, and hands it to every other game. A player whose emote is the sit, which every game reads from the player's saved data, sits too, whatever the switch shows. While a player sits, those bones are kept whenever the base layer shows a sitting pose settled, 1 s or more into the sitting state with nothing crossing over, and put back right after the game's animation whenever it shows anything else, the crossing back to the seat included, or a sitting state younger than that, as when the game sits a player on the ground down again inside the same state. The switch blinking off for less than half a second keeps the pose. The arms are left to the game's throw, or to the fishing motions when they are on.

The game plays the cast (`Throw`), the pull (`ReelIn`) and the stance of holding the rod (`Idle_Fishing`) on the same base animation layer as every sitting pose (`SitChair`, `Emote_sit`, `SitShip`, read from the player's animator), and a layer plays one state at a time, so a seated cast or reel showed nothing. With `SeatedMotions` on, Rodnarök shows them on the bones instead (`SeatedRodPose`). The three clips are read once per rig as still frames, at least 30 a second (`ClipFrames`): a copy of the animator's skeleton is made inside an inactive holder, so none of its scripts wake, stripped of everything but its bones and its animator, posed frame by frame by a Playables graph of its own and thrown away. Then, right after the game's animators have posed the characters, before any script of the frame reads a bone (`AfterAnimation`, placed into Unity's own loop after the physics step's animation and after the frame's), only the shoulder, arm, hand, finger and head bones are turned toward those frames; the hips, the spine and the legs keep the seat's pose, kept as above. The shoulders (or the upper arms on a rig without them) and the head are held as the clip has them toward the whole character, and the bones below them as the clip has them toward the bone they hang from: the seated chest leans otherwise than the standing one the clips were made on, so held toward the whole character the arms carry the rod as they do standing. Turning them there, rather than in a late script, keeps the line drawn from the rod tip and the cape from flickering: the player's animator may pose the character in the physics step, which some frames have and some do not, so scripts reading the bones before a late script would see the game's pose on some frames and the turned one on others. A bone the animator has not posed again since the last turn keeps it, so nothing is turned twice. The throw's wind-up is played from the start of the throw and held while drawing, the throw goes on from there as the cast goes out, the pull plays while reeling and the stance while the float is out (`SeatedPoses`). One motion crosses over into the next in a quarter second, and the motions as a whole fade in and out as long, the last one playing on as they fade (`SeatedBlend`); standing up lets go at once, leaving the game's own way up. Every game does this for every seated player it sees, reading the sitting from the game's seat switch, the rod from what is seen in their hand, the reel from their block and the float from its owner; the draw and the throw are known only to the player's own game, so others see the stance and the pull. `SeatedMotions` in section `Display`, each player's own and on by default, turns it off; the self-test switches it on for its sitting scenario and puts it back after.

## How the arrival works

When a float the player's own game cast lies in the water, the wait is rolled (`Schools`). When it is over, what comes is decided (`Arrival.Choose`). A fish of the world's own that takes the bait is called first, if this game steers it, it is within the float's reach (the game's 50 m) and the water is deep enough for it every 2 m of the way to the float: it goes for the float at its next choice, hears no noise while called, and nothing is spawned; once hooked or let go it is the game's again (setting `CallWorldFish`, on by default). Without one, a school comes only where the water allows its whole visit: a place 6 to 14 m out to come from, and a way out from the float through water deep enough for it all the way, 40 m long or to water 4.5 m deep to dive in. Where it does not, no school comes and a line on the screen says once that there is too little water here for a school and that fish of the world's own still bite. A school also comes only once the caster's last school has left, so casting again never stacks schools. Then the school appears out of sight beyond the float as seen from the one fishing, by the same measure a leaving fish is taken away by (`Arrival.StartOutOfSight`): at the nearest place, up to 60 m out and through water deep enough for its fish all the way in, where the whole school is 40 m from every player; else 6 to 14 m out where the water lets it swim 4 m under the surface. Its fish spread up to 4 m apart, facing every way. The rolled wait is its swim in (`Drift`), counted from the float landing: the game picks each fish's next place to swim around its home, and the school's home creeps from where it appeared to the float over that time, or longer on a long way in, so that the fish, cruising at their own speed up to 3 m/s, always swim half again as fast as their home: from 40 m that is 20 s for a Perch and 30 s for the slowest fish. Its fish wander up to 6 m around the home, and a school that started deep rises to its own depth as it comes. A school rising from deep leaps only near the end of the way in. A fish bites only once the swim is over and half the school is near, or 8 seconds after it whatever the school is doing. At the float each fish circles it within 4 m at its own speed, picking a new place to swim every 1.5 to 3 seconds, near the top of the depth it swims at. The saved home of each fish is the float from the start, so another game taking a fish over sends it straight there. Its stars and size come from the game's own level-up roll for that fish beside the float. Each fish is placed only where the water is deep enough for it. A fish of a quality the game lets leap (`m_jumpMaxLevel`) leaps a few times on its way in, and only where the water stays deep for 10 m ahead.

A school's fish never ends up as a free fish on land. The game picks up a fish lying near a player like any dropped item and lets one out of the water be taken by hand; the schools' fish are never picked up in passing, and any of them found out of the water over dry ground and not on a line, flopping or lying still, is taken away at once. The game writes a fish's next place to swim before it finds the water there too shallow and turns it down, and the fish swims for it until a later choice holds; and a place it takes it checks only there and halfway, so the way can cross a bank. A school's fish and a called one swim only for a place reached through water deep enough every metre of the way; otherwise the place it swam for before stands, so none is steered at a bank either way. One already in shallower water than it swims in may take any way that never gets shallower than where it is, so it swims back out rather than holding on to a place beyond the bank. The rod's catch hands a hooked fish over as in the game. Once the school's swim in is over and half of it is near the float, or 8 seconds after that, one fish is picked and let go for the bait a couple of seconds later; from there the game's own nibble and bite follow. No other fish of the school goes for any float. When the picked fish is hooked, the rest make a place 30 m out in open water their home, in the direction most away from the float whose water is deep enough for them every 2 m of the way, so never across a spit of land or toward the shore, a shorter way of 20 or 10 m where no 30 m one is, and swim there at the game's flee speed. When the float is gone, all of them do, diving where the water is 4 m deep or more, and each is taken away only out of sight: 40 m from every player or 4 m under the surface; one stuck in sight is taken away 30 seconds on. As a school only comes where it has a way out, that is a safety net for a fish caught on something, not how fish leave.

A school is steered by the game that spawned it. Should another player's game take a fish over, as happens when the one fishing walks far off, it swims as the game would swim it around the float; any game owning a marked fish whose float is gone takes it away. A cave fish, which the world never spawns in groups, rolls its stars by the world's general rule.

## Rod-only fish and other mods

The fish a bait draws can be caught only with the rod (`RodOnly`, `School.Catchable`). The game spends bait only when a fish is hooked and gives it back when the line comes in empty, so without this a bait would bring a school of any fish at no cost for any other way of catching fish to take. Spearfishing (Korppis, `org.bepinex.plugins.spearfishing`) is the case in mind: its prefix on `Projectile.OnHit` turns a fish hit by a spear, harpoon, arrow or bolt into a floating item or puts it straight into the inventory, for any fish.

Harmony runs every prefix of a method whatever an earlier one returns, so Rodnarök cannot keep another mod's prefix from running; it changes what that prefix sees instead. Its own prefix on `Projectile.OnHit` runs first (`Priority.First`, and before Spearfishing by name): if the hit is a fish a bait drew, read from the fish's saved float mark so every game agrees, then for the rest of that hit the game's `Projectile.FindHitObject` answers nothing for that fish. The game's own hit finds no fish either, which changes nothing in the game, as a projectile does nothing to a fish. The school is spooked: it takes no bait from then on, and every fish of it not on a line leaves, diving out of sight as a school does once its float is gone. A fish of the world's own is left to the game and to those mods.

This covers every mod that finds what a projectile hit through the game's lookup. A mod that catches fish another way, by its own physics query or by taking fish near a piece, is not covered. OdinArchitect's *Fish Trap* never touches a fish in the water: it is a station turning Fishing Bait into raw fish, and nothing of Rodnarök changes it. The school is only steered away by the game that owns its fish, which is the game fishing; a projectile from another player still catches nothing, but the school swims on in that case.

## Limits

A fish needs water as deep as its own swimming depth: the Tuna swims no shallower than 4 m, and no school comes where the water by the float is shallower than its fish swim. The Frosty bait is made from a Drake trophy, so the Northern Salmon is open from the Mountain on. Fish that come to a float belong to the game of the player fishing, and others see them as ordinary fish. Only a fish of the world's own that the caster's game steers can be called; one another player's game steers is left to it, and a school comes instead where the water allows. The calling reach is the game's 50 m, so the fish called may be one the player had not seen; it swims in where it can be watched. The too-little-water line shows only to the player who cast. Whether the water allows a school is judged by the depth of the fish the bait drew for that cast, so a bait for deep fish, such as the Tuna, is turned away in more places. A school needs either open water 40 m beyond the float or water 4.5 m deep near it, so a lake both narrow and shallow gets none. Forty metres and four metres under are judged out of sight; how far a fish shows depends on the water and the graphics settings. The 40 m are measured from every player loaded around the one fishing. A fish taking line out is sent away from the rod only by the game that owns it, which for a school's fish is the game fishing; a world fish owned by another player's game takes the line out without heading away. The game's own break still holds: a fish dragging the float 10 m past the line, faster than the line goes out, breaks it as in the game.

## Settings

One setting is each player's own and never synced: `CastHints` in section `Display`, off by default, makes each cast show a line in the game's top-left messages naming the bait's fish the water there fits, those that would need bigger or deeper water, or the fish of the world's own called (`CastHint`). The fish of a bait are rolled only among those the water fits, each as likely, so a bait two fish take never turns a cast away for the one that does not fit. The others are in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Rodnarok.cfg`, section `Fishing`, and are synced to every player by ServerSync (vendored, see `src/ServerSync/README.md`). The server's values win for everyone on it, and the entries are locked, so only an admin changes them in game. A player without Rodnarök cannot join a server that has it.

## Building and testing

`dotnet build` builds against the installed game and deploys into the Gale profile; `dotnet build -c Release -p:LibsDir=lib` builds against the metadata-only copies in `lib/`, as CI does. `dotnet test tests/Rodnarok.Tests` runs the tests of everything that needs no game, and the standards tests that read the source.

The in-game self-test is a plugin of its own (`selftest/`, `Rodnarok.SelfTest.dll`), built and deployed with `dotnet build selftest -c Debug` and never packaged. In a world, `/rodnarok selftest` runs it and writes `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.log`; words after it pick the scenarios whose names hold them, `list` names them all and `stop` ends a run. With the file `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.run` present it runs by itself a few seconds after a character enters a world, with the words the file holds.
