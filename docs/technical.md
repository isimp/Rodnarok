# Rodnarök technical notes

## How the game fishes

These are the game's rules Rodnarök builds on, read from Valheim 1.0.20.

A fish picks a new place to swim every 5 to 15 seconds and, each time, takes a float within 50 m with a one in ten chance. It counts as arrived when it is level with the hook on one ground axis and in height, the other ground axis left out, so a float can dip with the fish still metres away. The bite is one downward push on the float, about as big as a wave, and holding the line hooks the fish only within half a second of it. A fish nibbling at the wrong bait never comes back to that float. Fish flee from the noise a player makes: walking reaches 15 m, running and jumping 30 m, building 50 m, fading by 4 m a second.

A hooked fish takes turns struggling and resting. Reeling costs the fish's stamina a second times its quality, far more while it struggles (a resting Perch 3, a struggling Northern Salmon 60), and Fishing skill lowers it to a fifth. While it struggles the line comes in at half speed, and at no stamina the fish is lost. The line breaks when the fish drags the float 10 m past the reeled length or 30 m from the rod; holding never strains it, so in the game holding is always safe. A struggle lasts half a second up to its fish's longest, 1.5 s more per star, and a rest a second or two up to five.

Every fish takes exactly one bait. Fish spawn only in their own biome, never inside a player base and never near a player; the Tetra lives only in mountain caves.

## What Rodnarök changes

The bait table is built at runtime from the baits the game's own fish take (`BaitTable`), so a fish another mod adds with a bait of its own comes too. A bait two fish take brings each in equal share. With the server setting `BiomeFishOnBait` on, as by default, the game's plain `FishingBait`, the bait every biome's bait is made from, also brings every fish the world's spawn lists spawn in the biome the float lands in (`FishBook` reads each fish's biomes from the enabled entries of every spawn list), judged as a spawn judges its point, by the biome of the ground under it (`ZoneSystem.GetGroundData`): the Trollfish in the Black Forest, the Giant herring in the Swamp, the Grouper in the Plains, the Tuna, Coral cod and Pufferfish at sea, the Anglerfish and Pufferfish in the Mistlands, the Northern salmon in the Deep North and the Magmafish in the Ashlands, beside the Perch and the Pike that take plain bait anywhere. Each fish that fits takes an equal share. A biome's own bait still brings only its fish, in any water. The Tetra spawns only from the Mountain caves' own spawners, in no biome of the world, and keeps to its Cold Fishing Bait. The game lets a fish bite only a bait its prefab lists (`Fish.TestBate`), so with the setting on a fish takes plain bait where it lives at the chance its own bait has (`BiomeFishTakePlainBait`); the same rule picks the fish of the world's own a float calls, and the cast hint names these fish too.

A while after the float lands, the bait draws a school of its fish (`School`): as many as the world spawns of that fish together (a Perch comes in twos to sixes, a Northern Salmon in threes to tens), each size as likely, and two to four of a fish the world never spawns in groups, such as the cave fish. Each fish rolls its stars as the world's spawns do, and the game draws a fish 40% larger for each star. They leap on their way in, circle the float near the top of the depth they swim at, and one of them, each as likely, takes the bait, so the catch keeps the game's odds of stars. When it is hooked the others scatter. With `Schools` off a single fish comes. With `Bumps` on (off by default), the fish picked to take the bait bumps the float once or twice before it goes for the hook (`Bumps`): a pull of 12 m/s² for 0.25 s against the bite's 14 m/s² for a second, none sooner than 0.4 s after it is picked or later than 0.6 s before it goes, two at least 0.5 s apart. A bump is no nibble the game knows of, so reeling at it hooks nothing, and it drags the float, which startles a wary school.

Fish never leave the world by themselves in the game: each is kept in the world's save until caught. Rodnarök marks its own fish, takes them away out of sight once their float is gone, and any player's game takes away a marked fish it finds with no float, so none gather in the save. A fish of the world's own is never taken away. Rodnarök's fish are not moved by the game's own flight from noise, which would scatter a school the moment a player walks up; the world's own fish still flee from it. Instead a school is wary in a way of its own (`Wary`, setting `Wary`, on by default). Every frame, in the game that cast the float, the game's own noise rule is asked at the float and at the school's middle, with 35% of the range, as a fish hears under water: a player within that share of their noise (the game's walking 15 m, running and jumping 30 m, building 50 m, fading by 4 m a second, so about 5 m walking and 10.5 m running; sneaking, sitting and standing still make none) startles the school, which darts 6 m off the player at the game's fleeing speed and comes back once all has been quiet for 3 s. The float moving across the water faster than 0.6 m/s, measured over half seconds, while a fish of the school is within 6 m of it, as a short reel does, only makes the school flinch: a 2.5 m sidestep off the float, back once the float has lain still for 1.5 s. While off the float no fish of it goes for the bait. Frights add up over a cast, a startle 2 and a flinch 1, and at 8 the school leaves for good, diving out of sight as a school whose float is gone; so does it when the float keeps moving that fast for 2 s with the school near it or already flinched aside, as reeling it in does. A fish sidesteps straight away where the water stays deep enough for it, else turned 45° or 90° aside, and waits where it is where none of these is deep enough. The cast's own start is too soft to matter: an attack's 10 m around the one fishing has faded before a school comes near. Once a school is ready to bite it is shy as much as the shyer of two measures says: how close the one fishing stands across the ground (shyest within 4 m, not shy from 12 m) and how shallow the water is under the float (shyest under 1 m, not shy from 3 m). As it is first ready it gives up and swims off with a chance of 30% of its shyness, and otherwise hesitates up to 12 s, as long as its shyness, before a fish goes for the bait. Only the game that cast the float runs this, as it steers the school; another player is heard only by their noise reaching the float. The hearing share, the frights that make a school leave and the chance of giving up are the Normal level's here (see Difficulty). A school scared away for good tells the one fishing why in a line at the top left (`SchoolGone`): their steps or another player's, named, when the last fright was heard; reeling through the school when it was a flinch or the float dragged through it; too shy to bite close in or in shallow water, by the shyer of the two measures; or a shot when something other than the rod hit one of its fish. A dart or a flinch it comes back from says nothing.

A bite needs the fish within reach of the hook through all three axes (`Bite`): its mouth within 0.6 m of the point a metre under the float where the game sends a fish going for it. The game counts a fish at that point by two of its three axes and steers it at its own speed and turning rate, so a fast fish circles the point: a Pike, 5 m/s turning 100 degrees a second, 2.9 m wide, and a Tuna, 10 m/s turning 80, 7 m. So within 3 m of the point a fish going for the bait, of a school or of the world's own called, swims no faster than lets it turn inside half its distance to the point, and at the point inside half the reach: its turning rate in radians a second times half that distance, for most fish easing from about 2.6 m/s at 3 m to 0.5 m/s at the point, the last 3 m in about 2 s (`BiteApproach`); at its own speed again once it bites, is hooked, misses or leaves. A fish that missed is given the game's own mark of a bait it failed at (`m_failedBait`), so lingering slowly by the hook it does not nibble that float again. A bite from further off is held back, and the fish tries again as it swims closer. A bite pulls the float under for about a second, beyond the one short push the game gives it. The hook window is a setting, 1.5 s by default. The game's float takes a new nibble a second after the last (`FishingFloat.RPC_Nibble`), inside that longer window, which would dip the float a second time and start the window over; so while the last bite can still be hooked the float takes no other, from that fish or any. These apply to every fish at a float, a school's or the world's own. The crosshair ring (`Ring`, `RingDraw`, `CrosshairRing`) is one ring of one size from the bite to the end of the fight, made from the game's bow ring as a ring of its own, so it never looks like drawing the rod. After a bite its arc is full and white and drains clockwise toward the top, turning yellow, as the hook window runs out. While a hooked fish struggles the ring is red; while it rests, yellow. With tension its arc is the line's strain, filling clockwise from the top over a faint full track of the same colour (a second ring made the same way); without, the whole ring shows. While a bow or the rod is drawn it is hidden and the game's bow ring shows as ever.

Missed bites wear the bait down (`BaitLoss`, setting `BaitLost`, on by default). The game lets a fish bite the same bait again a second after a missed bite, for ever, and gives the bait back when the empty float is reeled in. In the game that owns the float, a bite neither hooked nor any longer within the hook window is a miss, and the fish that bit can no longer be hooked. Each miss on a float may take the bait, more likely each time: on Normal 10% for the first, 40% for the second, 90% for the third and 98% for every later one. Taken, the float is bare, a bite on it is refused, the game counts its bait as used so reeling it in gives none back, and its school, or the fish of the world's own called to it, moves on. A fish that missed has learned: its bites on that float are refused and the school sends another of its fish; once every fish of the school has missed, the school swims off and the bait is left, so reeling in gives it back; a fish of the world's own called to the float is the game's again after its one miss. A line at the top left says each time whether the fish only nibbled at the bait, nibbled while the school swam off, or took it. A bump is no bite and costs nothing.

Holding the line while the fish rests reels it in at the game's speed and cost. A struggling fish shows it at the surface (`Thrash`): the game wiggles it and now and then splashes with it, mostly unseen under the water; Rodnarök brings it up until its back breaks the surface and draws the game's splash for it about three times a second, on every player's screen, and stops when it rests; a hooked fish is held near the float in both. The game saves a struggle's length as a decimal number but its end as a whole 0, which it keeps apart, so every game, the game's own splashes included, went on reading a struggle after it ended; the game owning the fish now saves the end as a decimal too. While it struggles the line stalls: no line is gained, and it costs the `StallStamina` setting a second, the same for every fish, lowered by Fishing skill as reeling is (`Fight`).

Line tension is a rule of Rodnarök's own, since the game's line never strains (`Tension`, `Lines`, setting `Tension`, on by default). Holding the line through a struggle strains it, from none to snapped over `SnapSeconds` (1 s); a line strained to snapping breaks as the game breaks one, with the game's message and effect. Easing off lets the strain go over `EaseSeconds` (1 s), and while the fish rests it eases whether the line is held or not, so a rest never strains it. While the line is eased off in a struggle the fish takes line out at `RunSpeed` (2 m/s) less the strain still on the line (half as fast at half strain, none at snapping), so easing off in short taps that keep the arc partly filled loses less line than letting go, and heads away from the one fishing, swimming as fast as the line goes out, so it never drags the float beyond the line to where the game would break it; past the game's 30 m the line breaks as ever. Once the run ends, the game that owns the fish gives it back its own speed. `SnapSeconds` and `RunSpeed` are a Perch's (speed 3 in its prefab): with `PullBySpeed` (on by default) every other fish pulls by the fourth root of its prefab's speed against the Perch's, snapping a held line sooner and taking more line the faster it is. A Pike (5) snaps it after 0.88 s and runs 2.3 m/s, a Tuna (10) after 0.74 s and 2.7 m/s, an Anglerfish or Tetra (2) after 1.1 s and 1.8 m/s; the five fish of speed 3 pull as before. The fourth root keeps the game's order of fish with small steps between them, so a beginner can still land each: at the full speed a Tuna would take 10 m a second, more in one struggle than a Fishing 0 reel wins back in a rest, and even the square root left a one-star Tuna at Fishing 0 needing about half again the stamina of a fight without the pull. A fish of a mod whose prefab gives no speed pulls as a Perch. The game's own struggle and rest times and stamina costs stay as they are, so how tiring a fish is still follows the game. Only a short struggle can be held through; most, and every one of a fish with stars, need the line eased off once or more. The strain is kept by the game that cast the float, which runs the fight. Every line that breaks in the game that cast it is written to the log, saying whether the strain snapped it or the game's own rule broke it, with the line's length, the float's distance from the rod tip, the tip's height above the sea, the fish's distance from the float and whether it struggled, the strain, the hold, the stamina and whether the one fishing sat. Each struggle writes one log line as it ends (the fish rests, the line snaps or the line is gone): how much line the fish took in how many runs, how long the line was eased off and held, and the line's length; a run with the block button down that the game did not take as blocking (with what it was doing: attacking, dodging, building, overweight, a minor action or staggered) or with stamina out is counted apart.

A hooked fish shows its next struggle coming (`StruggleWarning`, `StruggleTells`, setting `StruggleWarning`, on by default). The game decides when the next struggle starts as one ends (`Fish.m_nextEscape`, a rest of the fish's `m_escapeWaitMin` to `m_escapeWaitMax`), on the game owning the fish; that game writes the time onto the fish's networked data as server time (`Rodnarok.struggleAt`), and the game owning the float writes the line's strain in steps of 0.05 (`Rodnarok.strain`), so every game shows the same. With more than 1.5 s to go the float lies still, so a still float always leaves that long. Within the last 1.5 s the float's look trembles toward the fish as at a bite (`FloatTremble`, its closeness standing for how restless the fish is, eased in from the first moment), never the float with its physics body. In the last 0.6 s before a Perch's struggle the rod line is drawn taut, straight from the rod to the float over the game's sag (`LineConnect` draws the sag the float sets each step), and quivers a little; a faster fish gives less of that moment by its pull (`Tension.Pull`, a Tuna 0.45 s, an Anglerfish 0.65 s) and each star takes a tenth off, never under 0.25 s. The line never changes at once: it draws from the game's sag to taut over 0.3 s, eased at both ends, sags back to the game's own over 0.6 s once the struggle is over, and its quiver swells and settles over 0.3 s. Through the struggle the line stays taut and quivers by the strain, from a fifth of its swing with none to its whole swing at snapping: the middle of the line swings up to 0.25 m aside and half that up and down, nine times a second, both ends held. A struggle starting at the hook comes at once, so there is nothing to warn of. Switched off, nothing shows and the float stands upright.

The rod's secondary attack heaves a hooked fish (`Heave`, `Heaves`, setting `Heave`, on by default). A rod is drawn as a bow, so the game reads the secondary attack (`SecondaryAttack` or `JoySecondaryAttack`) and passes the press to `Player.SetControls` once per press but does nothing else with it; Rodnarök takes the press there. In a rest a heave lifts the fish and its float 3 m toward the rod tip over 0.3 s (10 m/s, beyond the game's best reel of 6 m/s, so a heave and its wind gain line faster than reeling at any Fishing skill), for the rod's own secondary attack stamina, which the game's rod carries though it has no secondary attack (`m_secondaryAttack.m_attackStamina`, 20, as an iron sword's or a mace's secondary), charged as the game charges any attack (`Attack.GetAttackStamina`): raised by the gear's attack stamina modifier and the status effects, then lowered by a third at full Fishing skill, 20 at Fishing 0 and 13.4 at 100. A modded rod's own figure counts. The game that cast the float runs the lift, moving the fish and the float together and stopping both where the lift ends so neither coasts on, and keeps the line's length, so the line is longer than the way to the float and the game draws it sagging by its own rule. While the line is more than 0.3 m longer than that way, holding the line winds it in three times as fast as the game's reel, never past the float, and the resting fish's weight costs no stamina, only the game's hooked drain. A struggle starting with slack out jolts the line: 0.35 strain for every metre of slack beyond 0.3 m, so a whole heave's 3 m left unwound nearly snaps an unstrained line by itself and snaps any line already strained. A heave into a struggle lifts nothing and adds a quarter of the way to snapping. Without tension a heave into a struggle does nothing. The heave relies on the game that cast the float owning the fish, as the line's strain and a fish's run do. The rod shows the heave: with a rod the game's block pose is the raised reeling pose, drawn from an animation flag (`blocking`) that `Humanoid.UpdateBlock` sets only when the blocking state changes and every game sees. Through a heave Rodnarök sets that flag after the game's update: the rod snaps up for the 0.3 s lift and comes down after, or, held up already, dips for 0.15 s and comes back up; then the flag is the game's own again. A seated player's rod moves by Rodnarök's seated pose instead and shows no heave.

While a school is at the float and no fish bites yet, the crosshair ring tells how close the fish is (`ApproachRing`), from the moment the school comes until the bite or until it leaves: at the bite's size, white, over a faint track, its strength by that fish's distance from the float across the water, as the player sees it rather than by its depth: whole within 0.6 m, the reach a fish bites from, 15% at 1.5 m, the picked fish's hover range, and beyond, eased between by distance. The fish it follows is the one picked to bite while it hovers, the nearest of a wary school while it hesitates, or else the nearest of the school. The secondary attack does nothing before the hook. A *Wisplight*'s wisp guarding the float comes back to its wearer once the school has left the float with nothing hooked, scared away, too shy, driven off, done with a bare bait or shot at, as it does when the float is gone.

The water is calmed by the game's own *Item stand*, wall or horizontal, holding a Serpent trophy (`CalmStand`, `CalmStands`); Rodnarök adds no piece of its own. Put up, the trophy calms nothing. A tap of the use key on that stand switches its calm on or off: the game gives the plain tap on a stand holding an item no work, while holding the key still takes the trophy and the turn key still turns it. The switch is kept in the stand's own saved data under a key of Rodnarök's, flipped by the stand's owner at a player's call, so every player sees the same and it lasts; the stand's ward is asked first, as the game asks it, and a boss's stone, whose trophy cannot be taken, is left alone. Taking the trophy off switches the calm off, so the next trophy put up starts off. The stand's hover text says whether it calms the waters and offers the switch, the key named as the game names it; the words are plain text, since the game takes new words only through a private table it clears on a language change. Every game looks over its loaded stands twice a second, so a stand on a ship takes the calm along with that lag.

A calming stand lowers the waves through the corner values the game keeps for each 64 m water tile. The game shapes the waves of what floats and draws the water from the same four values, so both stay calm together, and a corner is lowered by where it is alone, so two tiles meeting at it always agree and no seam shows (`CalmWater`). The calmed area follows the tiles' corners and fades across a tile. A place lies at most 45 m from its tile's nearest corner, at the tile's middle, so the default reach of 48 m (`CalmReach`) calms at least one corner wherever a stand stands; near a tile's middle that corner's share of the waves there is small.

## Fishing while sitting

The game stands a seated player up on any attack or block press (`Player.SetControls`), cancels a drawn cast while the player sits on a seat (`Player.UpdateAttackBowDraw`), and lets nobody start an attack in the sitting pose (`Character.CanMove`). A player holding a rod (anything casting fishing bait) who sits on a chair, a bench or a boat's bench, or on the ground with the sit emote, and holds still, is seen as not seated by those three for the moment they ask (`SeatedFishing`), so the rod casts and reels and the player stays seated. Moving, jumping or crouching stands the player up as in the game. A ship's controls are not a seat for this.

The game throws the rod's cast along the way the body faces, not the aim (the rod's attack has `m_useCharacterFacing` on, its upward aim off), and turns the body toward the aim while drawing; a seat holds the body still, so a seated cast went straight ahead. A seated player's cast and the point it leaves the rod are turned round the character toward the aim (`SeatedAim`, `SeatedThrow`, on `Attack.GetProjectileSpawnPoint`): on the ground all the way, on a chair, a bench or a boat's bench up to 60 degrees to either side of the seat's facing, an aim further round casting at that edge. Sitting on the ground, drawing also turns the body toward the aim at the game's own turning speed (`m_turnSpeed`, `SeatedTurn`), so the throw is seen going that way; a seat keeps its sitter's facing. The cast is made in the game of the one casting and its float is seen by every game.

The game plays its throw and its pull on the same base animation layer as the sitting pose, standing the whole body up for them, and its sitting down again after; the throw cannot be left out, since the cast leaves the rod at an event inside its animation. So every seated player's hips, spine and legs are kept as the seat has them (`SeatedBody`, `SeatedBodies`), whatever `SeatedMotions` says. A player sits for every game alike: the game turns on an animator switch for the seat, one named `attach_…` for a chair, a bench or a boat's bench and `emote_sit` on the ground, and hands it to every other game. A player whose emote is the sit, which every game reads from the player's saved data, sits too, whatever the switch shows. While a player sits, those bones are kept whenever the base layer shows a sitting pose settled, 1 s or more into the sitting state with nothing crossing over, and put back right after the game's animation whenever it shows anything else, the crossing back to the seat included, or a sitting state younger than that, as when the game sits a player on the ground down again inside the same state. The switch blinking off for less than half a second keeps the pose. The arms are left to the game's throw, or to the fishing motions when they are on.

The game plays the cast (`Throw`), the pull (`ReelIn`) and the stance of holding the rod (`Idle_Fishing`) on the same base animation layer as every sitting pose (`SitChair`, `Emote_sit`, `SitShip`, read from the player's animator), and a layer plays one state at a time, so a seated cast or reel showed nothing. With `SeatedMotions` on, Rodnarök shows them on the bones instead (`SeatedRodPose`). The three clips are read once per rig as still frames, at least 30 a second (`ClipFrames`): a copy of the animator's skeleton is made inside an inactive holder, so none of its scripts wake, stripped of everything but its bones and its animator, posed frame by frame by a Playables graph of its own and thrown away. Then, right after the game's animators have posed the characters, before any script of the frame reads a bone (`AfterAnimation`, placed into Unity's own loop after the physics step's animation and after the frame's), only the shoulder, arm, hand, finger and head bones are turned toward those frames; the hips, the spine and the legs keep the seat's pose, kept as above. The shoulders (or the upper arms on a rig without them) and the head are held as the clip has them toward the whole character, and the bones below them as the clip has them toward the bone they hang from: the seated chest leans otherwise than the standing one the clips were made on, so held toward the whole character the arms carry the rod as they do standing. Turning them there, rather than in a late script, keeps the line drawn from the rod tip and the cape from flickering: the player's animator may pose the character in the physics step, which some frames have and some do not, so scripts reading the bones before a late script would see the game's pose on some frames and the turned one on others. A bone the animator has not posed again since the last turn keeps it, so nothing is turned twice. The throw's wind-up is played from the start of the throw and held while drawing, the throw goes on from there as the cast goes out, the pull plays while reeling and the stance while the float is out (`SeatedPoses`). One motion crosses over into the next in a quarter second, and the motions as a whole fade in and out as long, the last one playing on as they fade (`SeatedBlend`); standing up lets go at once, leaving the game's own way up. Every game does this for every seated player it sees, reading the sitting from the game's seat switch, the rod from what is seen in their hand, the reel from their block and the float from its owner; the draw and the throw are known only to the player's own game, so others see the stance and the pull. `SeatedMotions` in section `Display`, each player's own and on by default, turns it off; the self-test switches it on for its sitting scenario and puts it back after.

## How the arrival works

When a float the player's own game cast lies in the water, the wait is rolled (`Schools`). When it is over, what comes is decided (`Arrival.Choose`). A fish of the world's own that takes the bait is called first, if this game steers it, it is within the float's reach (the game's 50 m) and the water is deep enough for it every 2 m of the way to the float: it goes for the float at its next choice, hears no noise while called, and nothing is spawned; once hooked or let go it is the game's again (setting `CallWorldFish`, on by default). Without one, a school comes only where the water allows its whole visit: a place 6 to 14 m out to come from, and a way out from the float through water deep enough for it all the way, 40 m long or to water 4.5 m deep to dive in. Where it does not, no school comes and a line on the screen says once that there is too little water here for a school and that fish of the world's own still bite. A school also comes only once the caster's last school has left, so casting again never stacks schools. Then the school appears out of sight beyond the float as seen from the one fishing, by the same measure a leaving fish is taken away by (`Arrival.StartOutOfSight`): at the nearest place, up to 60 m out and through water deep enough for its fish all the way in, where the whole school is 40 m from every player; else 6 to 14 m out where the water lets it swim 4 m under the surface. Its fish spread up to 4 m apart, facing every way. The rolled wait is its swim in (`Drift`), counted from the float landing: the game picks each fish's next place to swim around its home, and the school's home creeps from where it appeared to the float over that time, or longer on a long way in, so that the fish, cruising at their own speed up to 3 m/s, always swim half again as fast as their home: from 40 m that is 20 s for a Perch and 30 s for the slowest fish. Its fish wander up to 6 m around the home, and a school that started deep rises to its own depth as it comes. A school rising from deep leaps only near the end of the way in. A fish bites only once the swim is over and half the school is near, or 8 seconds after it whatever the school is doing. At the float each fish circles it within 4 m at its own speed, picking a new place to swim every 1.5 to 3 seconds, near the top of the depth it swims at. The saved home of each fish is the float from the start, so another game taking a fish over sends it straight there. Its stars and size come from the game's own level-up roll for that fish beside the float. Each fish is placed only where the water is deep enough for it. Only a Tuna or a Northern salmon of a quality the game lets leap (`m_jumpMaxLevel`) leaps on its way in, at most every 3 to 6 s, with the chance the game gives its own fish by the hour (full at a quarter and three quarters of the day, none at noon and midnight), and only where the water stays deep for 10 m ahead; the game's own leap values are the same for every fish, so which fish leap is Rodnarök's choice, the two that leap in life.

Every fish coming to a float tells itself by a sign of its own on the way in (`ArrivalSign`, `ArrivalSigns`): a Perch small rings where a fish rises, a Giant herring a flicker of tiny rings, a Pike nothing until its school is within about 9 m of the float and then one swirl beside it, a Trollfish a hump of water surging up with spray off its front and a V wake, a Grouper single big bubbles bursting into a ring, a Coral cod a fizz of tiny bubbles, a Pufferfish domes swelling on the surface, an Anglerfish a green lure glow under the water, a Magmafish steam puffs, a Tuna a boil of short splashes, a Northern salmon a fin cutting the surface, and a Tetra a fast zigzag of glints; a fish another mod adds shows the Perch's rings. The game steering a school's fish, or a fish of the world's own called to a float, marks each on its networked data with the float it is coming to (`Rodnarok.coming`), from when it sets off until a fish bites, and the fish going for the bait with the same float (`Rodnarok.going`): a called fish all the way, a school's fish once it is let to; every game reads the marks and shows the signs, so every player at the water sees them. A float's coming fish show one sign together, on one path (`SignPath`): the game turns each fish to a new place every 1.5 to 3 s, so the sign does not follow any one fish but the school's centre, smoothed over 0.9 s, and every showing comes on that path. The bulge, the glow and the fin ride it, facing the way it moves and keeping that heading while it is still, and their wake is drawn where the path was over the last 1.6 s, spreading behind and showing only while it moves. Once the path is within 4 m of the float with no fish going for the bait the school is milling there and the signs go quiet: the travelling sign fades out over about half a second and one showing comes by the float every 2 s, for the bulge, glow and fin a slow ring. A fish going for the bait is followed in, smoothed over 0.25 s, the travelling sign back and a showing every 0.9 s. How near a school is goes by its nearest fish, from 30 m off to 4 m from the float, and on the way in the signs come from every 1.9 s far off to every 0.6 s close and grow from 45% to full strength. They are drawn on the surface in the light the game puts on the world each frame (`SignLight`): a crest of foam lit by the game's directional light (the sun by day, the moon it turns that light into at night) by its colour, strength and height above the horizon, plus the ambient light, at 90% and never under 20% brightness so a night sign still reads, nor under 1.15 times as bright as the sky the water mirrors (the game's fog colour), since under a low or weak sun the water looks as bright as lit foam, its hue kept either way; under it a trough in the ambient light's hue at 30%, drawn just inside a ring, beside a wake and as the Trollfish's hump, whose rim is lit on the side toward the light. A ring's crest is brightest where it faces the light across the water, from 55% facing away to full, and rings and wakes thin to 40% of their width as they spread. A sign takes the game's fog by its own mode, density and range from the camera, toward the fog's colour and fainter; the Northern salmon's fin is dark by day and edged in the crest's colour by night; the Anglerfish's glow is a real light 0.6 m under the water.

The fish a sign follows is the fish that touches the bait. From the cast until a fish is hooked the caster's game marks its float as claimed (`Rodnarok.claimed` on its networked data), and the world's other fish, in every game, leave a claimed float's bait alone (`Fish.FindFloat` gives them none), so no unannounced fish bites while a sign shows another coming. Until the fish picked to take the bait may go for the hook it hovers within 1.5 m of the float, where the quiet sign's slow showing comes, and is marked as going for it, the sign following it in, only once it may bite; meanwhile it hovers, slowing near it as it will for the bite, and a bump comes only while it is within 2 m, one still due in the last 0.6 s before it may bite being dropped.

The moments around a bite read on the water too (`FloatTremble`, `ScatterBurst`, `SignStars`). As the fish marked as going for the bait closes its last 1.5 m, every game tilts the float's look (the float's child objects drawn by a mesh, never the float with its physics body, so the hook and line are untouched) in twitches toward the fish: each noses it over in the first fifth and eases it back, up to 12 degrees at the 0.6 m reach a fish bites from, a twitch every 0.6 s far to 0.3 s close; the look stands upright again when the marks are taken at the nibble. A fish that flees, the rest of a hooked school, a school scared away or darting from a fright, has the place it fled from and the server time marked on its networked data (`Rodnarok.fledFrom`, `Rodnarok.fledAt`); every game watches the fish that came to a float within the last 10 s and, for a flee marked within 1.5 s, shows it darting off once: a ring where it was and a short V of a wake running 2.5 m away from the fright in 0.8 s, fast at first. The game takes the coming marks at the nibble, before the hook scatters the school, hence the watch. A starred fish (the game's item quality on the fish's networked data, 1 plain) tells itself: a school holding one shows 10% bigger and 15% bolder on the way in, the same for any number of stars, and a fish going for the bait shows by its own stars, 15% bigger a star up to 45% and 20% bolder, plain if it has none. The signs' rings, lines and spots are kept and used again rather than made with each showing (`PartPool`, 64 of each kind spare).

The Trollfish's hump (`TrollHump`) stands up from the water rather than lying on it, since from the shore a mark flat on the surface is seen edge-on and does not read. It faces the camera across the view, so each player sees it side-on: 0.7 m long seen end-on to 1.7 m swimming across, higher toward its front, its ends meeting the water. Every 0.9 s it surges from 0.13 m to 0.3 m, rising in the first quarter and settling, and through the first 35% of a surge spray ticks fly off its front; a fish barely moving raises it to a third. Its crest is a line lit by the game's light over a band in the trough's colour, fading toward the water, both riding the waves near the camera; stars, contrast, rough water and the quiet at the float apply as to every sign.
The signs belong to the water they are on (`WaterMood`). The game makes its waves as strong as the water's depth setting at a place times the wind (`WaterVolume.CalcWave` scales its wave sum by `Lerp(0, wind, depth)`, and a Serpent trophy's calm lowers that depth setting), and that product, 0 to 1, is the roughness a sign reads. On calm water a sign shows whole; rougher water shows it fainter, down to 45% of its strength, and from a roughness of 0.2 breaks each ring in two places, up to 85% gone there, each ring's breaks turned at random. Signs drift downwind with the game's wind, 0.35 m/s in the strongest, and steam leans with it as it rises. Within 40 m of the camera they rise and fall with the waves: each sign finds its body of water once, then reads the game's own wave sum directly (no physics search per point), a ring at 8 places round it and followed between, a wake at every fourth point along it, a dart at its three points; the bulge, glow and fin sit on the water at the path. Farther signs lie flat. The server setting `SignContrast` (0.5 to 2, 1 as made) sets how strongly the signs show for everyone: every part's strength is multiplied by it, never past whole, and above 1 the lines of rings, wakes, splashes and darts widen, up to half again at 2; a weaker contrast keeps them as wide. The Anglerfish's light shines by it too. Rough water's fading comes on top, so calm water reads clearest at any contrast, and a difficulty level leaves it as set.

A school's fish never ends up as a free fish on land. The game picks up a fish lying near a player like any dropped item and lets one out of the water be taken by hand; the schools' fish are never picked up in passing, and any of them found out of the water over dry ground and not on a line, flopping or lying still, is taken away at once. The game writes a fish's next place to swim before it finds the water there too shallow and turns it down, and the fish swims for it until a later choice holds; and a place it takes it checks only there and halfway, so the way can cross a bank. A school's fish and a called one swim only for a place reached through water deep enough every metre of the way; otherwise the place it swam for before stands, so none is steered at a bank either way. One already in shallower water than it swims in may take any way that never gets shallower than where it is, so it swims back out rather than holding on to a place beyond the bank. The rod's catch hands a hooked fish over as in the game. Once the school's swim in is over and half of it is near the float, or 8 seconds after that, one fish is picked and let go for the bait a couple of seconds later; from there the game's own nibble and bite follow. No other fish of the school goes for any float. When the picked fish is hooked, the rest make a place 30 m out in open water their home, in the direction most away from the float whose water is deep enough for them every 2 m of the way, so never across a spit of land or toward the shore, a shorter way of 20 or 10 m where no 30 m one is, and swim there at the game's flee speed. When the float is gone, all of them do, diving where the water is 4 m deep or more, and each is taken away only out of sight: 40 m from every player or 4 m under the surface; one stuck in sight is taken away 30 seconds on. As a school only comes where it has a way out, that is a safety net for a fish caught on something, not how fish leave.

A school is steered by the game that spawned it. Should another player's game take a fish over, as happens when the one fishing walks far off, it swims as the game would swim it around the float; any game owning a marked fish whose float is gone takes it away. A cave fish, which the world never spawns in groups, rolls its stars by the world's general rule.

## Rod-only fish and other mods

The fish a bait draws can be caught only with the rod (`RodOnly`, `School.Catchable`). The game spends bait only when a fish is hooked and gives it back when the line comes in empty, so without this a bait would bring a school of any fish at no cost for any other way of catching fish to take. Spearfishing (Korppis, `org.bepinex.plugins.spearfishing`) is the case in mind: its prefix on `Projectile.OnHit` turns a fish hit by a spear, harpoon, arrow or bolt into a floating item or puts it straight into the inventory, for any fish.

Harmony runs every prefix of a method whatever an earlier one returns, so Rodnarök cannot keep another mod's prefix from running; it changes what that prefix sees instead. Its own prefix on `Projectile.OnHit` runs first (`Priority.First`, and before Spearfishing by name): if the hit is a fish a bait drew, read from the fish's saved float mark so every game agrees, then for the rest of that hit the game's `Projectile.FindHitObject` answers nothing for that fish. The game's own hit finds no fish either, which changes nothing in the game, as a projectile does nothing to a fish. The school is spooked: it takes no bait from then on, and every fish of it not on a line leaves, diving out of sight as a school does once its float is gone. A fish of the world's own is left to the game and to those mods.

This covers every mod that finds what a projectile hit through the game's lookup. A mod that catches fish another way, by its own physics query or by taking fish near a piece, is not covered. OdinArchitect's *Fish Trap* never touches a fish in the water: it is a station turning Fishing Bait into raw fish, and nothing of Rodnarök changes it. The school is only steered away by the game that owns its fish, which is the game fishing; a projectile from another player still catches nothing, but the school swims on in that case.

## Difficulty

The server setting `Difficulty` picks one of five levels, in the steps of the game's own combat setting (`VeryEasy`, `Easy`, `Normal`, `Hard`, `VeryHard`), or `Custom` (`DifficultyLevels`). Normal, the default, is Rodnarök as it played before levels. A level sets, from Very easy to Very hard: the hook window 2.5, 2, 1.5, 1 and 0.5 s, the last the game's own; a Perch snapping a held line after 2, 1.5, 1, 0.75 and 0.5 s; a strained line unstraining over 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25 and 1.5 s; an eased Perch taking 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 m of line a second; holding through a struggle costing 1, 1.5, 2, 3 and 4 stamina a second; reeling in a resting fish costing 50% and 75% of the game's own stamina on Very easy and Easy and the game's own from Normal on (Rodnarök answers the game's `Fish.GetStaminaUse` with the fish's `m_staminaUse` by that share), and the game's own drain of 1 stamina a second for any hooked fish, 0.2 at Fishing 100, held or not, by the same share (the float's `m_hookedStaminaPerSec` and `m_hookedStaminaPerSecMaxSkill`, set from the float prefab's own figures before each step). Very easy schools are not wary and missed bites cost no bait. From Easy to Very hard a wary school hears 25%, 35%, 50% and 100% of the game's noise range, the last as the world's own fish do, leaves at 12, 8, 6 and 4 frights, and up to 15%, 30%, 45% and 60% of the shyest schools give up; the first four misses on a float take the bait at 5/20/60/90%, 10/40/90/98%, 20/60/95/100% and 40/90/100%. On Hard and Very hard the fish bumps the float before it bites. Picking a level writes its numbers into the entries it sets, `HookWindow`, `SnapSeconds`, `EaseSeconds`, `RunSpeed`, `StallStamina`, `ReelStamina`, `Wary`, `WaryHearing`, `WaryFrightsToLeave`, `WaryGivingUp`, `BaitLost`, `BaitLossChances` and `Bumps`, so a config panel shows what is played, and the config file is saved once after. Changing any of them by hand to other than the level's number switches `Difficulty` to `Custom`, which plays the entries as set, the level's other numbers kept as they stood (`DifficultyLevels.Matches`). On every start a level other than `Custom` writes its numbers again, so entries edited in the file while the level stays named are written over; a file meant to be played as edited names `Custom`. Values arriving from a server are taken as they come, since the server filled them by its own level; a player who is not an admin cannot change them there, as ServerSync locks them. The feature switches, the waits and the calm water are their own settings at every level. The self-test runs each scenario on Normal's numbers, whatever level the world plays at, except the two that play the levels: one sets each level and reads back the rules, the other fishes a Perch on Normal and Very easy for the reeling stamina and hooks bites past and within the window on Very hard and Very easy. Both need a world of the player's own, since a server's level wins.

## Limits

A fish needs water as deep as its own swimming depth: the Tuna swims no shallower than 4 m, and no school comes where the water by the float is shallower than its fish swim. The Frosty bait is made from a Drake trophy, so the Northern Salmon is open from the Mountain on. Fish that come to a float belong to the game of the player fishing, and others see them as ordinary fish. Only a fish of the world's own that the caster's game steers can be called; one another player's game steers is left to it, and a school comes instead where the water allows. The calling reach is the game's 50 m, so the fish called may be one the player had not seen; it swims in where it can be watched. The too-little-water line shows only to the player who cast, and so does the line telling why a school was scared away: a player whose steps scared another's school is not told. A server's `Difficulty` is everyone's on it; a player picks a level of their own only in a world without a server that has Rodnarök. Whether the water allows a school is judged by the depth of the fish the bait drew for that cast, so a bait for deep fish, such as the Tuna, is turned away in more places. A school needs either open water 40 m beyond the float or water 4.5 m deep near it, so a lake both narrow and shallow gets none. Forty metres and four metres under are judged out of sight; how far a fish shows depends on the water and the graphics settings. The 40 m are measured from every player loaded around the one fishing. A fish taking line out is sent away from the rod only by the game that owns it, which for a school's fish is the game fishing; a world fish owned by another player's game takes the line out without heading away. The game's own break still holds: a fish dragging the float 10 m past the line, faster than the line goes out, breaks it as in the game.

## Settings

One setting is each player's own and never synced: `CastHints` in section `Display`, off by default, makes each cast show a line in the game's top-left messages naming the bait's fish the water there fits, those that would need bigger or deeper water, or the fish of the world's own called (`CastHint`). The fish of a bait are rolled only among those the water fits, each as likely, so a bait two fish take never turns a cast away for the one that does not fit. `FloatWisp` in the same section, on by default and each player's own, has the wisp of a *Wisplight* the player wears guard their float (`FloatWisp`, `FloatWisps`). The game moves the wisp (`SE_Demister`) only in the wearer's own game, every physics step, toward a place 3.5 m over the wearer, with the *Wisplight*'s acceleration 7, top speed 80 and friction 0.03 a step, and snaps it there from beyond 12 m. While the wearer's float is out Rodnarök moves it right after the game does, the same way, toward a place by the float: searching, it goes round the float 1.1 to 1.5 m out at 0.7 radians a second, 1.05 to 1.75 m up, and every 4 s dips for 1.1 s to 0.4 m over the water; at a bite within the hook window it is drawn three times as hard to 0.45 m over the float, and its light, which the game flickers over its own intensity, flares to 2.2 times that intensity, fading to 1.3 times over the hook window; hooked, it is drawn twice as hard to 0.8 m over the float at 1.3 times its light. Once the float is gone it is drawn back half again as hard toward its place over the wearer and handed to the game's own following, its speed with it, within 4.5 m, the *Wisplight*'s own 6 m leash less the 1.5 m its place drifts by, so it never snaps across or is dragged. The wisp is a networked object whose place every game is sent, so every player sees it move; the flare is the wearer's alone, since only the wearer's game learns of a bite. The wisp's mist clearing (`Demister`, a force field the game's mist leaves clear) moves with it, so while it guards a float its networked data names the wearer, and every game keeps a copy of the clearing 3.5 m over the wearer, as wide as the wisp's own, and narrows the wisp's own to 6 m, as wide as the game's clearing where wisps gather; once the mark is gone, or the wisp or the wearer, the copy goes and the clearing is the game's own again. The others are in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Rodnarok.cfg`, section `Fishing`, and are synced to every player by ServerSync (vendored, see `src/ServerSync/README.md`). The server's values win for everyone on it, and the entries are locked, so only an admin changes them in game. A player without Rodnarök cannot join a server that has it.

## Building and testing

`dotnet build` builds against the installed game and deploys into the Gale profile; `dotnet build -c Release -p:LibsDir=lib` builds against the metadata-only copies in `lib/`, as CI does. `dotnet test tests/Rodnarok.Tests` runs the tests of everything that needs no game, and the standards tests that read the source.

The in-game self-test is a plugin of its own (`selftest/`, `Rodnarok.SelfTest.dll`), built and deployed with `dotnet build selftest -c Debug` and never packaged. In a world, `/rodnarok selftest` runs it and writes `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.log`; words after it pick the scenarios whose names hold all of them, in any order, `failed` runs again what failed or was skipped in the last runs (kept in `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.failed` until a run passes them), `plan` runs what the plan file asks for, `list` names them all and `stop` ends a run. Each scenario ends PASS, FAIL, or SKIP when the place the character stands in lacks what it needs, such as deep water in front or an out-of-sight spot beyond the float; a skipped scenario says what it needs, tried nothing of the mod, and counts apart from the failures. With the file `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.run` present it runs by itself a few seconds after a character enters a world, with the words the file holds. A session that changed something writes the plan, `BepInEx/Rodnarok-selftest.plan`, so only what the change touched runs: one line each, a word and a tab before it, `why` the change, `part` a scenario by name, `words` every scenario whose name holds the words, and `run on-load` to start it by itself on entering a world when no `.run` file is there. After every run the scenarios that passed come off the plan, skipped ones stay, and the file goes once nothing is left.
